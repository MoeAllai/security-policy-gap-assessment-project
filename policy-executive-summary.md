# Policy Executive Summary

## Purpose

This executive summary provides a leadership-level overview of the security policy gap assessment for CloudCore Analytics.

The goal is to summarize policy maturity, key gaps, risk impact, framework alignment, and recommended remediation priorities.

---

## Assessment Overview

CloudCore Analytics is improving its information security governance program and requested a review of its current security policy set.

The review focused on whether policies are complete, current, owned, approved, aligned with recognized framework concepts, and strong enough to support audit readiness and customer security reviews.

The assessment reviewed policies related to access control, MFA, incident response, vendor risk, data classification, business continuity, change management, acceptable use, and evidence retention.

---

## Overall Assessment

CloudCore Analytics has a basic security policy foundation, but the policy set is not fully mature.

Several policies exist and are approved, but the review identified gaps related to ownership, annual review dates, control requirements, evidence expectations, exception handling, and framework alignment.

The highest-risk gaps relate to access control, privileged access, MFA exceptions, incident response notification requirements, vendor risk management, restricted data handling, and evidence retention.

---

## Policy Maturity Summary

| Area | Current Maturity | Summary |
|---|---|---|
| Information Security Policy | Moderate | Policy exists but governance responsibilities need more detail |
| Access Control Policy | Needs Improvement | Access review and privileged access requirements need stronger language |
| Password and MFA Policy | Needs Improvement | MFA is required, but exception handling is unclear |
| Incident Response Policy | Needs Improvement | Customer notification expectations are not clearly defined |
| Vendor Risk Management Policy | Needs Improvement | High-risk vendor requirements are incomplete |
| Data Classification Policy | Needs Improvement | Restricted data handling rules need more detail |
| Acceptable Use Policy | Moderate | Policy exists but enforcement responsibilities need clarity |
| Business Continuity Policy | Needs Improvement | Annual BCP/DR testing requirement is not clearly documented |
| Change Management Policy | Moderate | Normal change process exists, but emergency change process needs detail |
| Evidence Retention Policy | Weak | Evidence naming, storage, retention, and ownership are not standardized |

---

## Key Findings

| Priority | Finding | Risk Level | Business Impact |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Access Control Policy does not require quarterly access reviews | High | Inappropriate access may remain active without review |
| 2 | Privileged access requirements are incomplete | High | Admin access may be granted or retained without proper justification |
| 3 | MFA exception process is not clearly defined | High | Authentication exceptions may weaken security controls |
| 4 | Incident response customer notification requirements are unclear | High | Customers may not be notified consistently or timely during incidents |
| 5 | Vendor risk policy does not define high-risk vendor requirements | High | Sensitive data may be shared with vendors before proper review |
| 6 | Restricted data handling requirements are not detailed enough | High | Sensitive data may be stored, shared, or transmitted improperly |
| 7 | Evidence retention standards are not standardized | Medium | Audit evidence may be difficult to locate or validate |

---

## Framework Alignment Summary

The identified gaps were mapped to NIST CSF and ISO 27001-style concepts.

| Framework Area | Main Related Gaps |
|---|---|
| Governance | Policy ownership, review cadence, approval history, evidence retention |
| Access Control | Quarterly access reviews, privileged access, access approval expectations |
| Authentication | MFA requirements and exception management |
| Incident Response | Customer notification and escalation requirements |
| Supplier Security | High-risk vendor review requirements |
| Data Protection | Restricted data handling and retention requirements |
| Business Continuity | Annual BCP/DR testing and evidence requirements |
| Change Management | Emergency change approval and post-review requirements |

---

## Recommended Remediation Priorities

Management should prioritize remediation in the following order:

1. Assign policy owners and annual review dates
2. Update Access Control Policy to require quarterly access reviews
3. Define privileged access approval, justification, and review requirements
4. Create a formal MFA exception process
5. Update Incident Response Policy with customer notification expectations
6. Update Vendor Risk Management Policy with high-risk vendor requirements
7. Strengthen Data Classification Policy for restricted data handling
8. Standardize Evidence Retention Policy and audit evidence requirements
9. Add annual BCP/DR testing requirements
10. Improve emergency change and acceptable use enforcement language

---

## Recommended Target Timeline

| Timeline | Recommended Action |
|---|---|
| 0–15 days | Assign policy owners and confirm policy inventory |
| 0–30 days | Update access control, privileged access, and MFA requirements |
| 30–45 days | Update incident response, vendor risk, data classification, and evidence retention policies |
| 45–60 days | Update business continuity, change management, and acceptable use policies |
| 60–90 days | Validate updated policies, collect approvals, and store audit-ready evidence |

---

## Business Impact

If these policy gaps are not remediated, CloudCore Analytics may face:

- Audit readiness issues
- Weak access control governance
- Inconsistent MFA exception handling
- Delayed or unclear customer notification during incidents
- Incomplete vendor risk reviews
- Poor handling of restricted data
- Difficulty proving compliance during customer security reviews
- Inconsistent evidence retention and documentation

---

## Final Recommendation

CloudCore Analytics should prioritize policy updates that make control expectations clear, measurable, enforceable, and auditable.

The recommended decision is to approve a policy remediation plan and assign ownership to Compliance, IT, IT Security, Privacy, Operations, HR, and Engineering teams.

After policy updates are completed, the company should perform a follow-up validation review to confirm that each policy includes ownership, review date, approval history, control requirements, exception handling, and evidence expectations.

---

## Analyst Conclusion

CloudCore Analytics has a useful security policy foundation, but stronger governance documentation is needed before future audits and customer security reviews.

The most urgent improvements are access control requirements, privileged access governance, MFA exception management, incident response notification language, high-risk vendor review requirements, restricted data handling rules, and evidence retention standards.

This assessment demonstrates how policy review and framework mapping can help an organization improve governance maturity and audit readiness.
