# Policy Scenario

## Mock Company

**Company Name:** CloudCore Analytics  
**Industry:** SaaS / Data Analytics  
**Company Size:** 120 employees  
**Assessment Type:** Security policy gap assessment  
**Mock Role:** Junior GRC Analyst  

CloudCore Analytics is improving its information security governance program. The company has several security policies, but leadership is concerned that the policies may be incomplete, outdated, or not clearly aligned with recognized cybersecurity frameworks.

The company wants to prepare for future audits, customer security reviews, and internal governance improvements.

---

## Business Problem

CloudCore Analytics has grown quickly from 40 employees to 120 employees. During that growth, security documentation was created quickly and informally.

Some policies exist, but they do not always include:

- Clear policy owners
- Review dates
- Approval history
- Control requirements
- Evidence expectations
- Framework alignment
- Exception handling
- Enforcement responsibilities

Leadership wants to understand where the current policy set is weak and what corrective actions should be taken.

---

## Assessment Objective

The objective of this project is to review the company’s security policy set, identify gaps, map the gaps to NIST CSF and ISO/IEC 27001-style concepts, and create a corrective action plan.

The project focuses on practical governance work that a Junior GRC Analyst may support during audit readiness, customer security reviews, or internal compliance improvement.

---

## Policies in Scope

The following policies are included in the review:

| Policy | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Information Security Policy | Defines overall security expectations and governance responsibilities |
| Access Control Policy | Defines user access, access reviews, and privilege management |
| Password and MFA Policy | Defines authentication and MFA requirements |
| Incident Response Policy | Defines how security incidents are identified, reported, and handled |
| Vendor Risk Management Policy | Defines third-party security review requirements |
| Data Classification Policy | Defines how sensitive data is classified and protected |
| Acceptable Use Policy | Defines acceptable use of company systems and data |
| Business Continuity Policy | Defines continuity and disaster recovery expectations |
| Change Management Policy | Defines how system changes are reviewed and approved |
| Evidence Retention Policy | Defines how audit and compliance evidence is stored and retained |

---

## Key Review Questions

The policy review is designed to answer the following questions:

1. Does each policy have a documented owner?
2. Does each policy have an annual review date?
3. Does each policy include approval or version history?
4. Are responsibilities clearly assigned?
5. Are access control and MFA requirements clearly documented?
6. Are incident response notification expectations defined?
7. Are vendor risk requirements based on vendor risk level?
8. Are sensitive data handling requirements clearly defined?
9. Are business continuity testing requirements documented?
10. Are audit evidence retention expectations standardized?

---

## Expected Outcome

The final output should help leadership understand:

- Which policies are missing key governance elements
- Which gaps create the highest business risk
- Which policy updates should be prioritized
- How the policy set aligns with NIST CSF and ISO 27001-style concepts
- What evidence is needed to prove policy governance during audits

---

## Final Assessment Direction

Based on the mock review, CloudCore Analytics has a basic security policy set, but the policies are not fully mature.

The recommended direction is to improve policy ownership, annual review cadence, approval tracking, access control requirements, MFA documentation, incident response notification language, vendor risk requirements, data classification handling, business continuity testing, and evidence retention standards.
