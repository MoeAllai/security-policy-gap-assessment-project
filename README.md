# Security Policy Gap Assessment Project

## Project Overview

This project simulates a Junior GRC Analyst assignment focused on reviewing security policies for a mock SaaS company and identifying gaps against common cybersecurity framework concepts.

The goal is to demonstrate practical GRC skills in policy review, gap assessment, NIST CSF mapping, ISO 27001-style mapping, corrective action planning, findings documentation, and executive reporting.

## Mock Scenario

CloudCore Analytics is a 120-employee SaaS company improving its information security governance program.

The company has several security policies, but leadership is concerned that the policies may be incomplete, outdated, missing ownership, or not fully aligned with recognized cybersecurity frameworks.

A Junior GRC Analyst is assigned to review the current policy set, identify gaps, map the gaps to framework concepts, and recommend corrective actions.

## Project Scope

The review covers the following policy areas:

- Information security policy
- Access control policy
- Password and MFA policy
- Incident response policy
- Vendor risk management policy
- Data classification policy
- Acceptable use policy
- Business continuity policy
- Change management policy
- Evidence retention policy

## Key Findings Summary

The review identified several governance and policy gaps, including:

- Policies do not consistently show ownership and review history
- Access Control Policy does not require quarterly access reviews
- Privileged access requirements are incomplete
- MFA exception handling is not clearly defined
- Incident response customer notification expectations are unclear
- Vendor Risk Management Policy does not fully define high-risk vendor requirements
- Data Classification Policy does not clearly define restricted data handling rules
- Business Continuity Policy does not clearly require annual BCP/DR testing
- Evidence Retention Policy does not standardize evidence naming, storage, and retention

## Framework Alignment Summary

| Framework Area | Related Focus |
|---|---|
| NIST CSF Govern | Policy ownership, oversight, evidence retention, vendor risk governance |
| NIST CSF Protect | Access control, MFA, data protection, acceptable use |
| NIST CSF Respond | Incident response notification and communication |
| NIST CSF Recover | Business continuity and recovery planning |
| ISO 27001 Concepts | Access control, supplier security, incident management, business continuity, documented information |

## Recommended Remediation Priorities

Management should prioritize the following improvements:

1. Assign policy owners and annual review dates
2. Update Access Control Policy to require quarterly access reviews
3. Define privileged access approval, justification, and review requirements
4. Create a formal MFA exception process
5. Update Incident Response Policy with customer notification expectations
6. Update Vendor Risk Management Policy with high-risk vendor requirements
7. Strengthen Data Classification Policy for restricted data handling
8. Standardize Evidence Retention Policy and audit evidence requirements
9. Add annual BCP/DR testing requirements
10. Improve emergency change and acceptable use enforcement language

## Analyst Conclusion

CloudCore Analytics has a basic security policy foundation, but stronger governance documentation is needed before future audits and customer security reviews.

The most urgent improvements are access control requirements, privileged access governance, MFA exception management, incident response notification language, high-risk vendor review requirements, restricted data handling rules, and evidence retention standards.

This project demonstrates how a Junior GRC Analyst can support governance maturity by reviewing policies, identifying gaps, mapping requirements to frameworks, and building a corrective action plan.
